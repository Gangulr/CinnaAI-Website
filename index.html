Cinnamon deserves better than guesswork

Cinnamon is one of Sri Lanka's most valuable export crops, yet growing and harvesting it still depends on manual experience.

Plant growth is not monitored in real time, diseases are found too late, farmers cannot accurately predict when a plant is ready, and bark extraction is slow and manual. That means low productivity, uneven bark quality, high labour cost and delayed harvesting decisions.

CinnaAI joins three parts into one platform for the Sri Lankan cinnamon industry: IoT monitoring of the growing environment, machine learning that predicts growth and harvest readiness, and an AI-guided robotic machine that harvests the bark.

How the system works

Data flows in one direction, from the plant to the machine.

  1. IoT monitoring

    Sensors track temperature, humidity, soil moisture, UV light and white light, and stream live data to a cloud dashboard.

  2. ML prediction

    Models use sensor and image data to predict growth, detect disease, estimate bark thickness and judge harvest readiness.

  3. AI vision

    Computer vision finds harvest-ready bark and its peeling boundaries, then calculates the target coordinates.

  4. Robotic harvesting

    A robotic machine follows the coordinates to peel the bark, with Start, Stop, Emergency Stop and manual override.
